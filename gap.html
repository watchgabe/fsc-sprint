01 / The problem

No hook and no set of visuals can save a bad idea

The hook gets somebody to watch. The visuals keep them around for a few seconds longer. A good original idea is what makes them follow, save it, send it to someone, and come back looking for you next week.

Topics are the 80/20 of this whole thing. Pick a topic the person you're trying to reach doesn't care about, and no hook in the world saves it. Pick the right one and even a mediocre hook works.

Great hook. Great lighting. Nobody cared.

If that has happened to you, the problem almost certainly sat upstream of the shoot. The idea was dead before the camera came out, and no amount of craft further down the line could bring it back.

What this costs you

  • A shoot day spent on something that was never going to land
  • An edit you hated doing because you could already feel it was flat
  • The confidence hit of posting something good and watching it do nothing

The fix is not working harder on the post. It is spending ninety seconds on the idea before any of that starts.

02 / The framework

GAP

A simple set of questions to decide whether your idea has the legs to be worth developing. If it fails one of these, it's probably worth remixing, reframing, or scrapping.

Three questions. Fail one, kill the topic.

  • G — Gap. Did they learn something they didn't know they were missing?
  • A — Anchored. Can you trace it to something you actually lived?
  • P — Practical. Can they DO something after watching?

Three ticks and it ships. Any cross and you either rebuild the idea until it passes, or you let it go and pick another one. Letting it go is not a failure. It's the whole point of having a filter.

03 / The first question

G Knowledge Gap

Did they learn something they didn't know they were missing?

A gap is the distance between what somebody believes right now and what's actually true. Closing it is the moment they stop scrolling, sit back slightly, and think about it for a second longer than they meant to.

  • Nobody needs another post that says "consistency is key"
  • You need to say something that makes them stop and think
  • Explain your own aha moments, because they can unlock the same feeling in someone else
  • If a smart person in your audience already knows it, kill it

How to find the gap in an idea you already have

Take the idea and write down the sentence your audience would say about it right now. Then write the sentence you'd say. If those two sentences are the same, there's no gap and you're about to spend a day agreeing with people. If they're different, the distance between them is your post.

Regurgitation is the most common reason a well-made post dies. The craft was fine. The idea was borrowed.

04 / The second question

A Anchored

Can you trace it to something you actually lived?

Anchored means the idea traces back to something that happened to you. Not something you read in a book, watched on YouTube, or heard on a podcast. It happened to you, or you were there, or you did the work.

  • If you can't trace it to a specific moment, it's floating
  • The easiest way to build credibility is through your experiences, losses, and lessons
  • Floating ideas are the ones anybody could have posted, which is why nobody remembers who did

The 5 Anchors

Every post should touch at least one.

  • Expertise  ·  what you know
  • Story  ·  where you came from
  • Humanity  ·  who you actually are
  • Aspiration  ·  what you're building
  • Service  ·  what you give away

Your unfair advantages

The raw material nobody else has access to.

  • Skills you've built over years
  • Taste, and the specific things you find good or unbearable
  • Personality traits, including the inconvenient ones
  • Your current circumstances, whatever they happen to be
  • Where you live and how that changed you
  • Experiences, especially the expensive ones
  • Friends, family, and the people in the room with you

This list is the reason two people can teach the same thing and only one of them gets followed.

05 / The third question

P Practical

Can they DO something after watching?

Practical means somebody watching can act. A prompt they can copy, a framework they can install, a step they can take this afternoon. Content that entertains without giving them an action gets consumed and forgotten by dinner.

  • A prompt they can use, a framework they can install, or a step they can take
  • Content that entertains without an action gets consumed and forgotten
  • Give them something they can use off the platform
  • If they walk away impressed but idle, add a "here's what to do" beat

The most common miss

This is the question people skip, and it's why genuinely good ideas underperform. The insight is real, the story is real, and then it ends. Adding one concrete instruction at the end is often the entire difference between a post people like and a post people save.

A saved post is somebody planning to come back and do the thing. That's the signal worth chasing.

06 / Run it on real ideas

Three topics, three verdicts

"Here's the exact system I use to shoot a month's worth of B-roll in one day."

✓ Knowledge Gap   ✓ Anchored   ✓ Practical

Ships. It closes a real gap, it's traceable to a system you actually run, and there's something to copy at the end.

"Personal brand is the only moat AI can't clone."

✓ Knowledge Gap   ✓ Anchored   ✗ Practical

Rebuild it. Strong insight, real conviction, and nothing for the viewer to do. Add a "here's what to do about it" beat and it passes.

"You have to post more content."

✗ Knowledge Gap   ✗ Anchored   ✗ Practical

Scrap it. Obvious, borrowed, and there's no action in it. Somebody with twenty posts and six hundred followers said this, and it wasn't theirs either.

07 / When an idea fails

Remix, reframe, or scrap

A cross is information, not a verdict on you. Three things you can do with it.

Remix it

Usually fixes a failed Gap.

The topic is fine, the angle is tired. Change who it's aimed at, invert the claim, or attach it to a framework of your own so it stops being the version everybody has already seen.

Reframe it

Usually fixes a failed Anchor.

The idea is good and it isn't yours yet. Find the moment in your own life where you learned it. Rebuild the post around that moment and let the lesson come out of the story instead of arriving first.

Scrap it

Usually the right call on a failed Practical you can't fix.

If you can't name a single thing somebody should do after watching, and adding one would feel bolted on, let it go. There are more ideas. Killing one costs ninety seconds. Shooting one costs a day.

09 / Feeding the filter

Where the ideas come from

A filter is only as good as what you put through it. Seven places to pull from, so you're choosing between ideas rather than reaching for whatever surfaced.

Internal

  1. Random thoughts, captured the moment they happen
  2. AI interviews, where you get interrogated on one question
  3. Coaching and client calls
  4. Your own winners, mined for what actually worked

External

  1. Long-form content from people worth learning from
  2. Competitor outliers, meaning their posts that beat their own average
  3. Scroll reactions, the things that genuinely annoy or move you

Record everything. Every conversation, every walk, every huddle. The brain you feed is the brain you get to mine later.

One rule that sits over all of it: AI amplifies. It never originates. You are the source. Everything you speak about should tie back to a real experience, a lesson learned, or a topic you have genuine authority in.
